# World Bible

## Project identity
- **Working title:** TBD
- **Genre:** Fantasy; soul displacement into the past
- **Status:** Early development

## Core premise
A magical researcher in his twenties from a highly advanced, magically industrialized society dies and finds his soul about three hundred years in the past, inhabiting the body of a recently deceased eighteen-year-old low-ranking noble.

Before his death, he has just graduated from the Imperial Military Academy. Born into a merchant family, he was separated from them at a young age because of his magical aptitude and compelled into the academy; the authority and exact mechanism behind this remain open. He has a natural aptitude for magical research and is well suited to it, despite the path into imperial service having been imposed on him. All officers graduating from the academy must spend their final six months of education as frontline soldiers on a military campaign, regardless of specialization. He is a capable swordsman, but hates killing on behalf of the Empire and celebrates graduation with fellow graduates partly because he expects to be done with frontline duty. He remains obliged to serve the Empire. Something goes wrong during the celebration and he dies; the incident, precise cause of death, displacement mechanism, and any causal connection between death and displacement remain undecided.

The protagonist retains the memories of both lives: his own life in the advanced original era and the host's life in the earlier era. He had studied the history of the New World, especially this region, because the small colonial port was his home city in the future. He recognizes the host as a historical figure: the duke's son who became the region's first baron. In the history he learned, the first baron died one year after arriving in the New World; the accepted explanation was that he drank himself to death because he could not bear separation from his family, or more cynically, from the family connections that enabled his dissolute lifestyle. He wakes in the small colonial port town that, about three hundred years later, will be the great metropolis where he grew up. The scale of the contrast is startling: the future city he remembers is, in this era, only a tiny settlement. The historical account says the first baron died a year after arrival, but the protagonist is now alive in that body beyond the point at which history says he died. History has already diverged; the old account describes the original timeline, not a guaranteed future for this one. Having two lives' worth of memories in one mind is a strange experience. The precise subjective experience and psychological effects remain to be defined; do not assume amnesia or a clean replacement of the host.

His future society is far more dystopian than the earlier era. The earlier era is an aristocratic kingdom just beginning to industrialize through magic, and an early precursor to the future Empire. There is no established world-ending catastrophe premise.

## The host and his family
The host was the eighteen-year-old son of a duke. He was an indulgent wastrel: he partied, drank, womanized, flaunted his rank, and used his family status to get what he wanted from other people. His behavior brought shame on his family.

He was once considered to have great magical potential and still possesses that potential, but he squandered it through indulgence and irresponsibility. His defining failure was wasting his ability, not lacking it. His father regards him as a disgrace and an embarrassment to the family.

The host died in his sleep after drinking too much alcohol. His exact medical cause of death is not established. In the original timeline, historical accounts say he died about a year after arriving in the New World, supposedly from drinking himself to death after being cut off from his family and its connections. That later historical death no longer describes the new timeline, because the protagonist now inhabits the host's body.

## The barony and frontier exile
The duke grants the barony to his son: a small barony on a newly discovered continent across the Vast Sea. The continent is newly discovered from the kingdom's perspective; it is not established to be uninhabited.

The duke does not want to abandon his son. Nevertheless, he believes he must distance him from the family because the son's conduct damages the standing and prospects of the rest of the family. The grant is a difficult, reluctant decision: it provides the son with a title, land, and responsibilities while removing him from the family environment. In practice, it amounts to exile on the frontier, but it is not simply abandonment.

The precise resources, borders, population, revenue, obligations, and authority attached to the barony remain open.

## Established setting facts
1. The protagonist is a magical researcher in his twenties from a highly advanced, magically industrialized future. Born to a merchant family, he was separated from them young due to his magical aptitude and compelled into the Imperial Military Academy. He is naturally suited to magical research, is a capable swordsman, and dislikes killing for the Empire. His final six months of academy education were spent as a frontline soldier on campaign, a requirement for all graduating officers. He dies during a graduation celebration, with the incident and cause undecided.
2. The future society is much more dystopian than the era to which he travels; no world-ending catastrophe is established.
3. He dies in his original era and his soul travels about three hundred years into the past, inhabiting a recently deceased eighteen-year-old. The cause of death, mechanism of displacement, and any causal connection between them are undecided.
4. The protagonist retains memories of both his original life and the host's life. Having both lives' memories in one mind is a strange experience; its precise psychological and experiential details remain open.
5. The earlier era is an aristocratic kingdom just beginning to industrialize through magic, and a precursor to the future Empire. The protagonist wakes in a tiny colonial port settlement that will become the great metropolis where he grew up about three hundred years later.
6. The host is the duke's son, an indulgent wastrel whose partying, drinking, womanizing, flaunting of rank, and abuse of family status brought shame on his family.
7. The host once had great magical potential and still possesses it, but squandered it through irresponsibility and indulgence.
8. The host died in his sleep after drinking too much alcohol; the precise medical cause is unknown.
9. The duke reluctantly grants his son a small barony on a newly discovered continent across the Vast Sea, both providing for him and distancing him from the family to protect the rest of the family.

## Open questions
- What makes the future dystopian, and how did its society become that way?
- How and why does the tiny colonial port settlement grow into the metropolis the protagonist remembers from childhood?
- What are the rules, costs, and limits of magic?
- How does the earlier kingdom develop into the future Empire?
- What goes wrong during the protagonist's post-graduation celebration, causing his death? What is the displacement mechanism, and are death and displacement connected?
- How do the two lives' memories coexist in the protagonist's mind? Do they feel like memories of self, memories of another person, or some unsettling mixture?
- What is the new continent like, and who already lives there?
- What are the protagonist's goals and the novel's central conflict?
- What does the son's barony include in practice: land, people, revenue, obligations, and authority?
- How does the family, household, and wider society respond to the host's reputation?

## Coherence notes
- Future knowledge does not automatically provide the materials, infrastructure, or institutions needed to reproduce advanced magic.
- Do not assume the future's dystopia is caused by magic or magical industrialization.
- Do not assume the newly discovered continent is uninhabited.
- The host's lack of achievement is not proof of a lack of magical ability; his potential remains real.
- Keep the duke's motive clear: he wants to protect the rest of the family from the damage caused by his son's behavior, while reluctantly providing for his son rather than simply abandoning him.
- The protagonist inherits the host's reputation as well as his body, memories, and magical potential. He knows the host's historical identity and the region's early history from his original-life studies. How he uses or responds to that reputation is not yet canon.
- The protagonist remembers both lives. Do not simplify this into amnesia, a clean replacement of the host, or access to only one life's memories.
